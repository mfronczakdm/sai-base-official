## Overview

This page is limited to accent modification for adults, mainly nonnative accents. An accent is not a communication disorder. Everyone has an accent, and accents are tied to identity and community. The aim is not to erase a dialect or disconnect a person from that community.

Accent modification is an elective service for people who want to change how they speak. A noticeable accent can still be intelligible. Listener judgments of accentedness, comprehensibility, and intelligibility are also shaped by bias.

- Accents are a difference, not a disorder.
- Accent modification is the preferred term. Accent reduction and accent elimination are not.
- People may seek services when they are often asked to repeat themselves, when attention shifts from the message to the accent, or when bias creates barriers at school or work.
- Speech-language pathologists may provide this elective service within their scope of practice and state licensure rules, including telepractice.

## Key issues

Service decisions:

- Assessment focuses on intelligibility and the sounds or prosody the person wants to change, not on removing an accent.
- Providers need skills in phonetics, phonology, connected speech, second-language learning, and cultural responsiveness.
- No accent is inherently better than another. Linguistic bias can still pressure someone to modify their speech.
- Speech-language pathologists should separate a communication difference from a communication disorder.
